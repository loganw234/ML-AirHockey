How to read the network

Left column, the inputs: the ten numbers the AI sees each sixtieth of a second, written beside each one: where the puck is and how it is moving, where its own mallet is, and where the other mallet is. It sees the table from its own end, turned round from yours, so its right is the left of your screen.

Middle column, the hidden neurons: each adds up every input times the weight on its line, adds a constant of its own (its bias), and squashes the total into the range −1 to +1. Blue circles are positive right now, orange negative, grey near zero. Training made each one pick out a situation, but nobody chose which; picking one shows what it tends to respond to.

Right column, the outputs: where the AI wants its mallet. Target x runs across the table and target y from its own goal (−1) to the centre line (+1). The cross on the table is that spot; the mallet chases it at its top speed.

A line is a weight: how strongly one neuron pushes the next. Blue pushes the same way (the next one rises when this one does), orange the opposite way. A thick line has a big weight, and a bright one is carrying a lot at this moment.

To experiment: pause, pick something, and change it. Each pick says, measured on the last ten seconds of play, how much it moves the AI's aim and which way. Try switching off a hidden neuron, or blinding the AI to the puck's speed, then resume and watch how it plays. Set the bottom player to "the top player as loaded" to watch your edited network play the original.
